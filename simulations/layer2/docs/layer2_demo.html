The Vicsek model

Each agent moves at constant speed. Every step it takes the average heading of all agents within a fixed radius (itself included), adds a random angle drawn evenly from −η/2 to +η/2, and moves that way. There is no leader and no central plan; each agent only needs its neighbours' headings.

Try a sweep. Order falls as the noise rises. In a flock this small the fall is spread over a range of noise; the larger the flock, the sharper it gets. (In the report, 8 agents show only a broad, shallow maximum in χ, barely above the level of random headings, and the peak grows taller and sharper with flock size.)

χ is N times the variance of Φ over recent steps. It peaks where order is about to break, which is where the flock reacts most to one member turning.

Gray–Scott reaction–diffusion

Two virtual chemicals on a grid. V makes more of itself by consuming U; U is fed in everywhere and spreads twice as fast as V; V decays. From a few seeds, spots, stripes and mazes form with no blueprint. The feed rate F and the removal rate k pick which.

Turing (1952) showed that chemicals that react and spread at different rates can form such patterns. Gray–Scott is one example, of the kind where one chemical feeds on the other; Sim 11 in the report uses the activator–inhibitor kind (Gierer–Meinhardt) to spread drones over an area, with the field computed centrally. There it spread the drones about as well as simple rules, not better. Fields laid down by ground robots for the drones to follow are part of the LIMINAL design but are not simulated yet.

Try the presets. Small changes in F and k give very different patterns. Each preset re-seeds the grid; patterns take about half a minute to fill it.

When is a search finished?

Agents explore an unknown floor. Each grid cell is unknown, seen free or seen wall. The unknown cells form separate regions; counting them is the Betti number β₀ of the unknown area.

An unknown region that touches seen free space is open: an agent can go and look. One surrounded only by walls is sealed: no sensor can see into it from outside. Here the sealed regions are the insides of pillars, but from the map alone a sealed region could also be a closed-off room, so it is reported rather than counted as searched.

The idea. A list of waypoints can be finished and still leave a corner behind a pillar unseen. The count of open regions does not depend on any plan: the search of this map is finished only when it reaches zero. It assumes the map is right; a missed sensor reading would hide a region. The agents here are points; real drones cannot pass gaps narrower than themselves, so in the report's Sim 12 the count of open regions never reaches zero, and the search instead stops when no frontier is left and hands rescuers the list of regions it could not see into.
